Vedic vs Western Astrology Differences

Zodiac System:
Western astrology generally uses the Tropical Zodiac, which is based on the seasons and the vernal equinox. Aries starts on the first day of Spring in the Northern Hemisphere.
Vedic astrology (Jyotish) uses the Sidereal Zodiac, which is based on the actual observable constellations in the sky.

Ayanamsa:
Because of the precession of the equinoxes, the Tropical and Sidereal zodiacs have drifted apart by about 24 degrees. This difference is called the Ayanamsa. This means a person's Sun sign in Western astrology is often one sign earlier in Vedic astrology.

Focus:
Western astrology tends to be more psychologically oriented, focusing on personality, character traits, and individual potential.
Vedic astrology is traditionally more predictive and karmic, focusing on life events, destiny, timing (dashas), and remedies (upayas).

House Systems:
Western astrology uses various house systems (Placidus, Koch, Whole Sign).
Vedic astrology primarily uses the Whole Sign house system, where the Ascendant sign becomes the entire 1st house.

Lunar Nodes:
While both use the North and South Nodes (Rahu and Ketu), Vedic astrology places extreme importance on them as shadow planets causing eclipses and representing deep karmic desires and past-life mastery.
